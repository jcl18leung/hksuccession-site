The Succession Problem

Retirement should not mean closing the doors.

Across Hong Kong, many profitable businesses are still operated by the founders who built them decades ago. Their companies have employees, customers, supplier relationships, operating knowledge, licences and reputations that took years to establish.

Yet when the founder is ready to retire, the next generation may have chosen a different path. Senior employees may not have the capital to acquire the business. Traditional brokers may simply advertise the company and wait.

The result is a succession gap: good businesses can become stranded between the small-business marketplace and institutional M&A.

“The objective is not merely to sell a company. It is to create a credible transition from one owner to the next.” Second Wind
What We Look For

We focus on businesses worth preserving.

Second Wind is deliberately selective. We look for established companies where responsible ownership succession can preserve real economic value for customers, employees and the founder.

01

Genuine Succession Need

The founder is considering retirement or reducing involvement, and there is no obvious family successor prepared to take over.

02

Boring, Stable & Proven

We prefer necessary businesses over fashionable ones: specialist suppliers, maintenance firms, technical contractors, certified trades and other durable B2B services.

03

Locally Defensible

The business relies on trust, physical execution, certification, responsiveness, technical knowledge or customer relationships that cannot simply be outsourced across the border.

04

Built for the Next Decade

We seek companies whose products or services should remain economically relevant over the next 10–15 years, rather than businesses dependent on short-lived trends.

The Process

We turn an owner-operated company into an investable opportunity.

01

Understand the Business

We begin with the operating reality: customers, margins, employees, supplier relationships, owner responsibilities, competitive advantages and succession risks.

02

Package the Story

We organise financial and operational information, surface the company's real strengths and present them in a format that serious acquirers can evaluate efficiently.

03

Qualify the Buyer

Sensitive information is not circulated indiscriminately. Prospective buyers should demonstrate credible intent, financial capacity and an appropriate reason for acquiring the company.

04

Drive the Transaction

We coordinate information, anticipate diligence questions, maintain momentum and help both sides move toward a clear, executable decision.

The Operator's Edge

I have sat on the buyer's side of the table.

Second Wind was built around a simple idea: succession is easier when the person guiding the process understands what it means to actually acquire and operate a business.

Operator first. Intermediary second.

I have acquired businesses from retiring owners and understand the questions that rarely appear in a financial model.

Will the employees stay? Will customers remain loyal? Can the company operate without the founder? What knowledge exists only in someone's head? What will the first 100 days look like?

I also understand the seller's concerns: protecting employees, preserving reputation, choosing the right successor and avoiding an unnecessarily aggressive or disruptive sale process.

  • Direct experience acquiring from retiring founders
  • Practical preliminary due diligence
  • Hands-on preparation of buyer materials
  • Respectful communication with traditional business owners
  • Active transaction management rather than passive listing
